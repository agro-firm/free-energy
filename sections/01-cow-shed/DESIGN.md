# Cow Shed — Functional and Interior Design

## 1. Design goal

The interior is designed as a compact, high-clarity working shed where a worker can immediately understand:
- where feed enters
- where cows stand
- where water is available
- where manure moves
- where milk exits
- where equipment is serviced

The visual language should remain simple, durable, washable and agricultural rather than decorative.

## 2. Functional zones

### A. Central feed alley
- 6 ft planning width
- dry route
- connects to feed-delivery direction
- no manure equipment crosses this zone
- lighting focused on inspection and feeding
- floor finish textured but trolley-friendly

### B. Mangers
- 1.5 ft planning bands
- smooth, non-porous feed surface
- rounded/finished edges
- no exposed sharp steel
- designed for easy sweep/wash

### C. Cow platforms
- 5.5 ft planning depth
- 10 modules per side
- each module ≈ 4 ft wide in the planning grid
- robust galvanized dividers/rails
- non-slip textured concrete or approved resilient finish
- two end stalls can be temporarily adapted for treatment/calving as allowed by livestock professional

### D. Dirty/service lanes
- 3 ft planning bands along outside edges
- automatic scraper/manure function
- washdown capability
- access to downstream waste route
- utility fixtures protected from animal impact

### E. End cross/service zones
- 4 ft planning length at both ends
- cross movement and inspection
- scraper-turn/service access where equipment requires
- final gate/opening layout coordinated with whole-site plan

## 3. Interior visual character

Recommended:
- light-color reflective roof underside where feasible
- galvanized steel structure
- concrete lower surfaces
- simple black/galvanized stall rails
- visible color-coded utility labels
- high-contrast safety markings around moving scraper machinery
- minimal enclosed surfaces to maximize ventilation

Avoid:
- decorative false ceilings
- timber in constantly wet manure zones
- hidden conduits that are impossible to inspect
- floor trenches crossing the feed alley unless unavoidable
- exposed electrical joints within cow reach

## 4. Animal comfort design

The final livestock specialist should verify:
- stall size for actual breed/body mass
- neck rail/restraint choice
- flooring grip
- hoof-friendly transitions
- water availability
- fan airspeed
- shade/solar heat control
- lighting
- safe treatment access

The architecture provides room for these systems but does not replace specialist animal-welfare design.

## 5. Ventilation design

Use three layers:
1. **Open-side natural ventilation**
2. **Ridge exhaust/stack ventilation**
3. **Mechanical circulation fans** where measured climate data or cow heat load requires

Fans should blow along or across cow rows without obstructing roof solar maintenance or creating dead zones.

## 6. Lighting design

Concept:
- daylight through open sides
- LED fixtures mounted above cow reach
- even light over both rows and central alley
- local maintenance light at scraper drives
- emergency/egress light at exits

Avoid glare directly into cow eyes and avoid low hanging fixtures.

## 7. Water fixture design

Troughs should:
- be reachable by cows without blocking feed alley
- be removable/serviceable
- have isolation valves
- have overflow routed to controlled drainage, not across dry feed floor
- be protected against impact

## 8. Milking interface

The cow shed is not the milk-storage room.

The design only provides:
- hygienic cow access/connection point for milking system
- short clean route to Section 03
- power/water interfaces if milking equipment requires them

Milk should move away from manure handling as directly as possible.

## 9. Manure interface

Manure is handled at the outer dirty lanes.

The design principle is:
```
animal platform → dirty lane/scraper → downstream waste receiving
```

Feed does not cross this path.

## 10. Safety design

Provide:
- guards at scraper drive mechanisms
- emergency stop where powered scraper is controlled
- no exposed sharp corners
- clear worker escape path
- anti-slip transitions
- protected electrical equipment
- safe roof-access system for solar maintenance
- signage for moving machinery and wet floor

## 11. Interior maintenance philosophy

Every interior component should be:
- washable
- inspectable
- replaceable
- reachable without dismantling unrelated systems

The shed should not contain permanent storage piles. Storage belongs in Section 05.

## 12. Image consistency cues

When rendering the interior:
- always show 2 cow rows
- always show central 6 ft feed alley
- keep dirty lanes outside the cow rows
- show roof structure and ventilation
- show water troughs and utility lines
- do not show gas holder, gas treatment or generator inside
- show adjacent zone glimpses only where camera angle permits

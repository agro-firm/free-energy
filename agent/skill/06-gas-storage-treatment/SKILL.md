# Gas Storage + Treatment Skill

## Canonical order
Digester → primary condensate protection → raw gas holder → inlet knockout → lead H₂S vessel → lag H₂S vessel → fine coalescing/moisture separation → particulate polish → gas flow meter → low-pressure booster/regulator as required → generator.

## Section 09 storage baseline
- Gas production: 8.10–9.18 m³/day.
- Nominal raw-gas holder: 8 m³.
- Zone: 10 × 12 ft.
- Baseline technology: flexible H₂S-compatible membrane in ventilated protective frame.
- Main storage control is by holder volume/position.
- 75% demand-enable: 6.0 m³.
- 20% low-cutout: 1.6 m³.
- Usable swing: 4.4 m³.

## Section 10 treatment baseline
- Zone: **8 × 10 ft**.
- Design gas flow: **3 m³/h peak concept**.
- Raw H₂S sizing assumption: **2,000 ppm**, sensitivity **1,000–3,000 ppm**.
- Treated H₂S planning target: **≤100 ppm**, preferred **≤50 ppm** when required by generator vendor.
- H₂S media: **two 25 kg vessels in lead/lag series**.
- Media-capacity planning assumption: **0.10 kg H₂S/kg media**; actual supplier breakthrough capacity governs.
- Bulk water removal before media; fine moisture removal after media.
- No untreated bypass to generator.
- Low-pressure gas booster/regulator included only as needed to satisfy generator inlet pressure without pulling Section 09 into vacuum.
- Gas quality is verified with upstream/downstream sample points and downstream H₂S monitoring.

## Pressure rule
Do not adopt generic public pressure values as project setpoints. Holder vacuum limit, treatment pressure drop, booster suction/discharge and generator inlet pressure are coordinated from the selected membrane, scrubber and generator vendors.

## Media rule
Media replacement is based on measured H₂S breakthrough, pressure drop and vendor capacity—not calendar time alone.

## Safety
- methane/H₂S detection
- ventilated/open skid
- condensate closed drainage
- bonding/earthing
- hazardous-area review
- no open flame
- no untreated bypass

## Rule
Never route untreated raw biogas directly to the generator.
